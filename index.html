    Why 85% founder, 15% team pool

    Pre-funding, the founder is carrying every risk the company has: unlimited personal liability in practice, the capital already put in, the idea and the original plan, and the fact that if this fails tomorrow, the team moves on to another job while the founder's name and time are the ones sunk into it. An 85/15 split is a common, defensible starting point at this stage — it rewards that asymmetric risk without pretending the team's contribution doesn't matter.

    Two things worth knowing before you lock this number in:

    • It isn't permanent. If you raise outside funding later, a new investor's stake dilutes everyone's percentage proportionally (founder and team alike) — it doesn't eat into the team's 15% specifically.
    • It's a target, not a grant. Nobody should receive their full computed percentage on day one — see Vesting below.

    What a "point" actually is

    A point is not a mood score or a manager's gut feeling. It's the output of one fixed formula applied the same way to everyone, every time:

    Score = Priority × Type × Quality × Initiative × Efficiency

    Every entry's exact calculation is visible to whoever looks at it — click View on any row in the Work Log and you'll see the same five numbers multiplied together, in order, with the final total. Nothing is hidden in a black box.

    Priority — how important was it

    LevelBase points

    Type — task vs. project

    TypeMultiplier

    A Project is scored on its own merits — it is not the sum of its child tasks. If you also log the individual tasks inside a project separately, score the project for the ownership/coordination of the whole thing, not as a duplicate of the task totals.

    Quality — the only subjective input

    RatingMultiplier

    3★ ("met expectations") is the neutral baseline, not a punishment. This is the one number a reviewer judges — everything else in the formula is mechanical.

    Initiative — ownership bonus

    Who started itMultiplier

    Spotting and fixing something nobody asked for is a genuinely different signal than doing what you were told — it's rewarded, but modestly, so it can't be gamed by inventing busywork.

    Efficiency — estimated vs. actual hours

    This is deliberately the smallest lever in the formula. An uncapped "speed" score is the easiest part of any system like this to game — rush the work or lowball the estimate and the number goes up. Capping it both ways means finishing fast earns a small bonus, finishing slow costs a small amount, and neither can swing the result on its own. If no estimate is logged, it simply has no effect.

    Deductions — what loses points

    CategoryDefault

    Notice the deliberate gap between the two deadline categories: missing a deadline after telling someone in advance costs far less than missing it silently. That's intentional — the system is built to reward honesty about being late over hiding it, because the second behavior is the one that actually damages a small team.

    A member's points can't go below zero for equity purposes — deductions can wipe out a period's gains, but they can't put someone in negative equity. The raw (possibly negative) number still shows on their row for the record.

    From points to equity %

    1. Add up every scored work-log entry for a person → Gross points.
    2. Subtract every deduction on their record → Net points (floored at zero).
    3. Divide their net points by everyone's combined net points → their share of the pool.
    4. Multiply that share by the size of the team pool → their equity %.

    This always adds up to exactly the pool size — if there's ever a fraction of a percent left over from rounding, it's added to whoever has the largest share, so the visible adjustment is as small as possible and the total is never "approximately" 15% — it's always exactly 15%.

    Vesting — the part most first-time founders skip

    The equity % on the live sheet is a target — what someone would eventually hold if they stay the full course. Vesting decides how much of that target they'd actually keep if they left today:

    • Leave before the 12-month cliff → 0%, regardless of points earned.
    • Leave after the cliff but before month 48 → a straight-line proportion (e.g. 24 months in = 50% of their target locked in).
    • Stay the full 48 months → the full computed percentage.

    Without this, a real and common failure mode plays out: someone earns a meaningful points-based allocation, leaves after two months, and keeps all of it forever — "dead equity" that doesn't represent anyone's current effort and can scare off a future investor. The cliff and schedule exist specifically to prevent that.

    What "transparency" means here — and what it doesn't

    This page has no login and no per-person permissions — whoever can open the file can edit everything in it. That's fine for a founder's own working tool; it is not something to hand to the whole team as a live, editable page.

    "Transparent" is meant to mean: the formula above is open, every entry's calculation is inspectable, and every deduction carries a written reason. In practice, share it as: this explanation (so the method is never a mystery), each person's own exported row or a printed Plan Agreement (so they see their actual number), not the raw editable file itself.

    One honest limit

    This tool gets you to a fair, explainable number. It is not a substitute for actual paperwork. Before any of this becomes real ownership, get a CA or company secretary to set up the proper instrument — typically an ESOP for non-founder team members in an Indian company, or direct (sweat) equity shares if that's the route you choose, each with its own compliance and tax treatment. This tool decides who gets what; a professional should decide how it's legally issued.

    Ideas worth considering next

    Not built into the tool yet — each of these is a genuine option with a real trade-off, not an obvious upgrade. Pick deliberately rather than adding all of them.

    Peer recognition / kudos

    Let teammates flag a colleague's good work (not rate their own). A small, capped bonus for someone who gets multiple unprompted shout-outs in a period catches good work a founder alone might miss, and it starts to correct for single-rater bias — right now you are the only person scoring Quality.

    Trade-off: needs a way for each person to submit something without needing to log in as themselves. Keep the bonus small and capped so it can't become a popularity contest.

    Point decay for very old work

    Weight recent contribution slightly more than work from a year ago, so the split reflects who is driving the company now, not just who joined earliest.

    Trade-off: this can feel unfair to someone who did real heavy lifting early and then intentionally moved to a steadier pace — decide if "recency" or "cumulative effort" is the value you actually want to reward before turning this on.

    Minimum equity floor

    Guarantee everyone on the roster at least some small % (say 0.5%) once they've logged any reviewed work at all, so a quiet early contributor never shows literally 0.0%.

    Trade-off: floors have to come from somewhere — either the pool caps slightly below 15% in reserve, or the floor is funded by trimming the largest shares. Decide which before adding it.

    A standing quarterly review ritual

    The tool computes a number; it doesn't create the habit of looking at it. Put 20 minutes on the calendar every quarter to walk through the Work Log and Deductions together (as a process review, not a public shaming session) before generating the next Plan Agreement.

    New hires joining after the first agreement

    Decide now, in writing, whether a new team member later draws from a freshly-reserved slice of the pool, or whether the next agreement re-splits the whole 15% from scratch including them. The second option quietly dilutes everyone who's already vesting — most teams prefer the first.

    Export a per-person statement

    A "my numbers" view — one teammate's own entries, deductions and running total, exportable as a clean one-page PDF via the browser's own Print dialog — is a lighter-weight way to give someone their own transparency without handing over the whole editable ledger.
